# Response Decision & Safety Policy Engine (Step 4D)

## 1. Purpose
The Response Decision & Safety Policy Engine acts as the definitive authorization boundary before any response action is executed. While Step 4C (Optimizer) determines the most effective mathematical trade-off, Step 4D evaluates whether that action is legally, procedurally, and operationally permitted under the organization's security policy.

## 2. Policy Boundary
Step 4D does **not** execute actions or runbooks. It takes the output of the analytical layers (Steps 4A–4C) and applies safety and operational constraints. It is deterministic, rule-based, and heavily reliant on clear inputs. The output dictates whether an action can proceed automatically, requires human approval, or is outright denied.

## 3. Decision States
The policy engine outputs one of the following decisions:
- `ALLOW`: The action passes all policy checks and can proceed automatically (if `auto_approval_enabled` is true).
- `REQUIRE_APPROVAL`: The action is viable but requires human oversight due to target criticality, uncertainty, blast radius, or default policy.
- `DENY`: The action violates a hard constraint (e.g., target is protected, action is denylisted).
- `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`: The analytical layers did not provide enough data to safely evaluate the policy.

## 4. Policy Precedence
When multiple policy rules conflict, the engine uses strict precedence:
1. `DENY` takes ultimate precedence. If any rule evaluates to `DENY`, the final decision is `DENY`.
2. `REQUIRE_APPROVAL` takes precedence over `ALLOW`.
3. `ALLOW` is only granted if no rule requires approval or denies the action.

## 5. Allowlist / Denylist
The engine only permits actions explicitly listed in `allowed_actions`. Any action not allowlisted is automatically denied. Furthermore, if an action exists in `denied_actions`, it is explicitly blocked regardless of optimizer score.

## 6. Target Eligibility
The context of the target asset or identity heavily influences the decision:
- **Protected targets**: Resources flagged as `protected` (e.g., core security services, break-glass accounts) trigger an immediate `DENY`.
- **Tenant Isolation**: Any mismatch between the organization ID of the input data and the target or policy results in a hard `DENY`.

## 7. Production and Critical-Asset Rules
Even highly recommended actions may require human intervention if they target vital infrastructure:
- If a target is marked as `production` and `production_requires_approval` is enabled, the action is marked for approval.
- If a target's criticality is `CRITICAL` and `critical_asset_requires_approval` is true, human approval is mandated.

## 8. Confidence Rules
The policy relies on the confidence scores generated by earlier steps:
- **Activation Confidence**: Must meet `minimum_activation_confidence`.
- **Prediction Confidence**: Must meet `minimum_prediction_confidence`.
- If confidence falls below these thresholds or uncertainty is explicitly `HIGH`, the action is escalated for approval.

## 9. Simulation Completeness
Step 4B provides the simulated impact. If the simulation state is `PARTIAL`, human approval is required. If the simulation had `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, the action is unconditionally denied.

## 10. Blast-Radius Constraints
A hard limit on operational disruption is enforced via `maximum_blast_radius`. If a candidate's blast radius exceeds this threshold, the action is denied.

## 11. Reversibility Constraints
Actions that cannot be easily undone (low reversibility score) will trigger a `REQUIRE_APPROVAL` state, ensuring potentially destructive actions are scrutinized.

## 12. Auto-Approval
Even if all policy constraints pass, `ALLOW` is only granted if `auto_approval_enabled` is explicitly `True`. Otherwise, the system defaults safely to `REQUIRE_APPROVAL`.

## 13. Policy Versioning
Every decision records the `policy_id` and `policy_version` that were active at the time of evaluation. This guarantees historical auditability even as policies evolve.

## 14. Expiration
For actions that require approval or are allowed, an `expires_at` timestamp is generated. This ensures that old authorizations cannot be executed indefinitely if environmental conditions change.

## 15. Provenance
Decisions explicitly trace evidence back to its origin (`OBSERVED`, `SIMULATED`, `INFERRED`, `POLICY`). The final policy decision logs all triggered rules and their explanations.

## 16. Safety Limitations
- The policy engine is purely analytical and completely decoupled from cloud execution APIs.
- It relies on the accuracy of the Security Digital Twin and prior analytical steps. Missing or `UNKNOWN` business impact defaults to requiring approval.

## 17. Future Approval Workflow
Step 4D concludes the analytical intelligence phase. Step 5 will handle the integration with ticketing systems (Jira, ServiceNow), external approval tokens, and actual safe execution.
